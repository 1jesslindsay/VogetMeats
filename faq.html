FAQ

Quality You Can Taste, Service You Can Trust

Hours & Visiting

What are your store hours?

October – December: Monday–Friday 8am–5:30pm, Saturday 8am–4pm.

January – September: Tuesday–Friday 8am–5:30pm, Saturday 8am–4pm.

We are closed on Sundays year-round.

Do we need an appointment to drop off meat or a carcass?

No appointment is necessary to drop off boneless meat. However, due to space limitations, carcass drop-off is by appointment only.

Drop-off hours: 8am–4pm on weekdays and 8am–2pm on Saturdays.

Processing & Custom Orders

Do you process game animals from other states?

Yes. However, Oregon state regulations require that carcasses coming from states with documented cases of chronic wasting disease (CWD) must have the entire spinal column removed prior to transport. These regulations apply to deer, elk, and moose.

Is there a minimum weight for a separate batch of sausage?

Yes. For stand-alone separate batches, we require a 25 lb minimum. If you are short, we can sell you boneless meat to reach the minimum.

How much extra does a separate batch of sausage cost?

We charge an extra $0.40 per pound for separate batches. If your batch is 60 lbs or larger (per product), the extra charge is waived.

Do you sell sausage casings and seasonings?

Absolutely. We ask that you call 10–14 days in advance to reserve the quantity you need.

Do you slaughter or process beef, pork, or lamb carcasses?

No — we are not a slaughter facility, and we do not process beef or lamb carcasses. However, we do sell ½ or whole hogs cut to your specifications, as well as BBQ hogs.

Does your cured meat contain nitrates or nitrites?

Yes. To prevent the growth of botulism (Clostridium botulinum), all our cured meats contain small amounts of nitrites (about 120 ppm). Most products made by other companies that are labeled "uncured" contain either celery juice or powder, which also contains nitrates or nitrites that prevent the growth of botulism.

Storage & Preparation

What is the best way to cook Voget's ham?

Our ham cooking instructions are passed down from the company's founder, Garfield Voget.

For best results, bake our ham in the brown paper sack provided at purchase. Heat the oven to 200°F, place the ham inside the paper bag, and set on a baking pan or rack.

  • Whole ham: 9–10 hours, or until the internal temperature reaches at least 152°F.
  • Half ham: 6–7 hours, or until the internal temperature reaches at least 152°F.
  • Boston Butt: 3.5–5 hours, or until the internal temperature reaches 152°F.
How long can I keep smoked meat in the refrigerator or freezer?

We suggest that all smoked meats be frozen if not used within 10 days of purchase. For best quality, smoked meat should not be frozen for more than 6 months.
